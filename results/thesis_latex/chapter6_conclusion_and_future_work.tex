\chapter{CONCLUSION AND FUTURE ENHANCEMENTS}
\label{chap:conclusion}

This thesis addressed a fundamental operational bottleneck in contemporary Internet of Things (IoT) cybersecurity: machine learning based Network Intrusion Detection Systems (NIDS) deployed on resource-constrained edge gateways are invariably trained under a static, closed-world assumption. While conventional detectors achieve near-perfect classification on familiar network traffic, they fail catastrophically when confronted with previously unobserved attack variants and zero-day exploits. On memory-constrained edge hardware, conventional remedies break down: periodic full retraining on historical data archives exhausts on-device memory and causes prohibitive retraining delays, whereas naive incremental updates induce severe catastrophic forgetting of earlier threat signatures.

To resolve this challenge, this research designed, implemented, and empirically validated a resource-efficient, two-stage continual intrusion detection framework. By decoupling passive traffic inspection from on-demand supervised retraining, the framework harnesses an unsupervised Isolation Forest novelty screener to flag suspicious deviations in unlabelled traffic streams, prompting an analyst verification step that triggers Stage~2 continual adaptation. At the core of the adaptation engine lies the volume-aware Dynamic Replay rule, which analytically couples the historical exemplar retention quota to the incoming attack volume within strict hardware-enforced memory bounds ($< 1\text{ MB}$).

This chapter synthesizes the overarching empirical findings, articulates the core technical insights governing continual learning on tree ensembles, documents the rigorous verification procedures ensuring experimental validity, provides actionable deployment guidelines for edge practitioners, and outlines promising future research avenues.

The empirical findings yield five fundamental technical insights:

\subsection{Why Headline Accuracy Deceives in Edge Intrusion Detection}
On the known-class evaluation partition, the baseline Random Forest achieved an outstanding $99.02\%$ accuracy and $0.9890$ Macro-F1. When evaluated on the mixed test stream containing unseen zero-day MITM attacks, overall headline accuracy remained seemingly exceptional at $97.17\%$. However, this aggregate metric masked complete structural failure: the detector correctly identified zero MITM flows ($F1 = 0.0000$). Because MITM traffic represented less than $2\%$ of the total evaluation stream, missing every single zero-day intrusion lowered aggregate accuracy by only $1.85\%$. In mission-critical edge deployments, reporting aggregate accuracy on closed-world benchmarks creates a perilous false sense of security. Class-wise F1 and unweighted Macro-F1 must serve as the primary evaluation criteria.

\subsection{The Dual Operational Hazard of Misclassification}
Analysis of the $10 \times 10$ confusion matrix demonstrated that an unobserved attack causes collateral operational damage beyond simple evasion. In our experiments, $29.26\%$ of unseen MITM flows were misrouted to \texttt{normal}, allowing malicious traffic to penetrate the IoT subnet completely unhindered. Simultaneously, $39.85\%$ of MITM flows were misclassified as \texttt{dos}, contaminating security alerts, causing human operator fatigue, and degrading DoS precision from $0.989$ to $0.903$ ($F1$ drop from $0.9855$ to $0.9411$). Consequently, unobserved threats actively undermine trust in the detection of familiar classes.

\subsection{Unsupervised Anomaly Screening as an Edge Adaptation Trigger}
The unsupervised Isolation Forest achieved an $18.86\%$ novelty recall on unobserved MITM flows, while flagging only $2.70\%$ of familiar attacks and $6.92\%$ of normal flows. This represents a sevenfold enrichment in anomaly concentration. While an $18.86\%$ recall is insufficient for an autonomous standalone blocking system, it provides an ideal operational trigger for continual adaptation: capturing nearly $200$ anomalous exemplars into a quarantine buffer is more than sufficient for security analysts to construct a verified adaptation batch, initiating Stage~2 without continuous on-device retraining overhead.

\subsection{Why Class-Balanced Replay Outperforms Full Archive Retraining}
Under extreme data scarcity ($M = 207$ MITM flows), naive full historical retraining yielded a disappointing $F1_{\text{new}}$ of $0.6483$ with an unacceptably low recall of $47.96\%$. This empirical failure is directly explained by CART decision tree mechanics. In Gini impurity optimization:
\begin{equation}
\Delta I(s) = I(s) - \frac{|s_L|}{|s|}I(s_L) - \frac{|s_R|}{|s|}I(s_R),
\end{equation}
candidate splits are weighted by node sample proportions. When $207$ novel flows are submerged inside an archive of $127{,}787$ historical samples (a minuscule $0.16\%$ presence), splits separating the rare threat provide negligible global impurity reduction near the root, causing trees to subsume the novel class into majority classes. Conversely, class-balanced replay ($k = 100$ exemplars per class) equalizes effective priors ($100$ per old class vs.\ $207$ for the new class), raising MITM recall to $95.55\%$ ($F1 = 0.9773$, a $+0.3290$ gain) while slashing storage by $99.30\%$ and accelerating training by $11.6\times$.

\subsection{Mitigating Volumetric Surge Forgetting via Dynamic Replay}
While a static quota of $k = 100$ excels on rare attacks, it exhibits severe vulnerability under volumetric attack surges. When an incoming attack arrives with thousands of flows (e.g., $M = 2{,}903$ for XSS), the static new-to-buffer imbalance ratio surges to $\rho = 3.23$, causing maximum forgetting $\mathcal{F}_{\max}$ to jump to $0.3972$. Crucially, per-class case studies revealed that the worst-case degradation invariably strikes the smallest historical class (MITM, dropping from $0.7778$ to $0.3806$ F1). By dynamically scaling the per-class quota via $k_{\text{dyn}} = \lfloor M/K \rfloor$, Dynamic Replay re-establishes parity at decision splits, slashing maximum forgetting by $20.0\text{--}35.9\%$ across all volumetric families, preserving $>96.7\%$ memory savings, and executing updates in under $0.09\text{ seconds}$.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}
While the empirical results demonstrate robust statistical patterns, three methodological boundaries must be acknowledged:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Single-Benchmark Scope:} All experiments were conducted on the TON\_IoT network benchmark. Although TON\_IoT represents a modern, highly regarded testbed incorporating heterogeneous IoT protocols, evaluating cross-dataset transferability on corpora such as Edge-IIoTset remains essential.
    \item \textbf{Single-Step vs.\ Multi-Step Continual Learning:} Experiments evaluated a single incremental adaptation step ($K \to K+1$). In production networks, gateways encounter sequential streams of novel threats ($K \to K+2 \to K+N$).
    \item \textbf{Hardware Execution Environment:} Wall-clock latencies were benchmarked on a standard modern CPU. While relative speedups ($8\times\text{--}14\times$) remain invariant, absolute execution times on low-power ARM microcontrollers will scale proportionally.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Future Enhancements}
\label{sec:future_enhancement}

Building upon the theoretical and empirical foundations established in this thesis, five key directions are identified for future exploration:

\subsection{Sequential Multi-Class Incremental Learning ($K \to K+N$)}
In long-term deployments, gateways must assimilate multiple novel attack families arriving sequentially over months or years. Future work should investigate dynamic buffer pruning mechanisms (such as reservoir sampling or class-aging decay) to manage the global buffer ceiling $B_{\max}$ when the number of supported classes $K$ expands continuously.

\subsection{Asymmetric Minority-Aware Buffer Allocation}
In our volumetric experiments, the worst-case forgetting consistently afflicted MITM because of its small natural support. Developing an asymmetric allocation policy that assigns higher exemplar quotas to historically rare or overlapping classes could eliminate the remaining $0.22\text{--}0.25$ forgetting margin observed on minority classes.

\subsection{Semi-Supervised Active Learning and Analyst Assistance}
To minimize human analyst labeling overhead in Stage~2, active learning frameworks combining density-based clustering (e.g., HDBSCAN) with label propagation can be integrated. Analysts would only need to label cluster medoids, with automated propagation labeling the remainder of the adaptation batch.

\subsection{Hardware-in-the-Loop Embedded Prototyping}
Porting the framework to embedded IoT gateway platforms (such as Raspberry Pi 4, NVIDIA Jetson Nano, or ARM Cortex-A industrial routers) using lightweight runtime engines (such as ONNX Runtime or Treelite C-code compilation) will provide direct measurements of microsecond inference latency, cache utilization, and milliwatt power consumption.

\subsection{Cross-Dataset and Cross-Testbed Benchmarking}
Extending evaluation across complementary IoT benchmarks, including Edge-IIoTset and CIC-IoT-2023, will benchmark framework robustness across diverse industrial protocols (Modbus, MQTT, CoAP) and varying network topologies.

\vspace{0.5cm}
In conclusion, this research demonstrates that maintaining robust, lifelong network defense on constrained IoT gateways does not require hoarding massive historical datasets or executing computationally prohibitive retraining cycles. Rather, effective continual intrusion detection is achieved through intelligent, volume-aware memory management. By pairing lightweight unsupervised anomaly screening with a volume-matched Dynamic Replay rule, edge devices can autonomously adapt to unobserved cyber threats in fractions of a second, preserving sub-megabyte memory budgets while safeguarding previously mastered defenses.
